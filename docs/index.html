01Overview

Studies Apply Criteria at Different Points of One Lattice

The Platonic Representation Hypothesis (PRH) holds that increasingly capable models converge toward a common representation. However, recent studies have shown different views on aligning representations via their neighborhoods, spanning trees, and distances.

Lattice Representation Hypothesis (LRH)

In the lattice of alignment criteria, the set of criteria accepting two models describes what is shared by their representations. Studies have conflicting conclusions because they apply criteria at different points of this lattice. For independently trained models, the training procedure and the objective decide the criteria met reliably across runs, each in a different part of the lattice.

An Increasing Link Leaves Every Neighbor Set Unchanged and Can Lower CKA

In the animation, the squared distances of model B are those of model A passed through the increasing link \(g_t\) of Theorem 3, which keeps the order of all distances.

Every neighbor set and the spanning tree therefore stay unchanged while CKA falls below one, so this pair of models can satisfy a neighbor criterion and fail a CKA criterion.

02The Lattice of Alignment Criteria

Neighbor Sets and Spanning Trees Lie on Two Branches below the Order of All Distances

What a Criterion Is

An alignment criterion is a test that accepts a pair of models if one relation among shared evaluation samples, such as the neighbor sets in mKNN or the distances in CKA, agrees across the two models within a tolerance.

Stronger and Weaker Criteria

We call one criterion stronger than another if every pair accepted by the first is accepted by the second, and the criteria then form a lattice.

Scale and Protocol

The numbers \(k\) of neighbors and \(m\) of samples set the scale of a criterion, i.e., the range of distances over which the two models are compared. The protocol of a criterion fixes the preprocessing, the combination of the scores of all layer pairs into one score, such as their maximum, and the calibration.

One pair of models can satisfy a criterion on one branch and fail a criterion on the other.

03Four Studies on One Lattice

Conflicting Conclusions Can Come from the Scale, the Relation Compared, or the Protocol of a Criterion

PRHPlatonic Representation Hypothesis (Huh et al., 2024)

Agreement on All Distances Is Claimed, and Neighbor Overlap Is Measured

The convergence claimed by PRH amounts to agreement at the top of the lattice, whereas its evidence comes from mKNN, a criterion near the bottom that compares neighbor sets.

ARHAristotelian Representation Hypothesis (Gröger et al., 2026)

The Loss of the CKA Trend Can Come from Calibrating a Maximum over Layers

ARH restricts the claimed convergence to local neighborhoods because calibration keeps the size trend of mKNN but largely removes that of CKA. We show that this removal can come from calibrating a maximum over layers, whose chance level grows with effective dimension, so global convergence remains open.

Measurements in Section 05

BPCBack into Plato’s Cave (Koepke et al., 2026)

Neighbor Overlap Is Determined by Boundary Crossings at the Scale \(k/m\)

BPC infers coarse shared structure from the collapse of mKNN on larger evaluation sets. At a fixed \(k\), a larger evaluation set draws the boundary of the \(k\) nearest neighbors closer to each sample, so this collapse is at least partly a scale effect.

Measurements in Section 05

SOGStructure over Geometry (You et al., 2026)

Spanning Trees Contain the Nearest-neighbor Forest

SOG infers converging global structure from the size trend of MST overlap. Every MST contains the edge from each sample to its nearest neighbor, and these edges make up about 88% of the shared tree edges on the studied vision–language pairs, so MST overlap mostly repeats one-nearest-neighbor overlap on these pairs.

04What Determines the Criteria Met Reliably across Runs

The Training Procedure Decides the Criteria Met Reliably in a Linear Autoencoder, and the Objective in a Finite Contrastive Gibbs Model

Studies also claim that networks trained with different objectives learn similar representations, so we ask which criteria two independently trained models meet reliably, i.e., with high probability over their training runs.

Training procedureTheorem 2, linear autoencoder

The Training Procedure Selects the Gauge of a Linear Head

Under gradient flow, the initialization of a linear autoencoder selects its gauge, i.e., an invertible change of the representation that its linear head undoes, so two runs disagree on distances at every sample size. Weight decay or tied weights remove this disagreement as the samples grow.

Measurements in Section 05

ObjectiveTheorem 3, finite Gibbs model

The Objective Decides Whether Contrastive Learners Agree beyond the Order of Distances

In a finite contrastive Gibbs model whose population limit is the idealized contrastive learner of PRH, learners trained with InfoNCE and with the spectral contrastive loss learn one relation through different increasing functions. As their training samples grow, the two learners reliably agree on the order of all distances, whereas CKA between them tends to a limit below one.

Neighbor Overlap at a Fixed \(k\) Stays Flat or Falls with the Evaluation Size According to the Model Pair

Illustration in the BPC card

At \(k = 10\), the median mKNN of the vision–language pairs falls from 0.091 to 0.0085 between 256 and 16,384 images, whereas at the same ratio \(k/m\) the medians for \(k = 10\) and \(k = 100\) agree within 4%.

Shared Spanning-tree Edges Are Mostly Nearest-neighbor Edges, and Pretrained Vision and Language Representations Differ by More than a Monotone Link

Illustration in the SOG card

Nearest-neighbor edges make up a median 0.875 and 0.889 of the shared MST edges at mid depth and at the last layer. Beyond an affine fit, a fitted monotone link explains a further median of at most 0.0012 of the discrepancy between the squared distances of the two models.

The Threshold of a Calibrated Layer Maximum Can Be Forecast from Single-model Features

Trends in the ARH card

In all four families, the calibration threshold of the layer maximum follows the largest random-pairing baseline, and on LLaMA-1 calibration turned the CKA trend from 0.68 into −0.85, a drop predicted from single-model features.

The Training Procedure Decides Whether Two Runs of a Linear Autoencoder Agree on Distances

Illustration in the Theorem 2 card

Under gradient descent without a regularizer, 0 of 48 pairs of runs agreed on neighbor sets, mean-normalized squared distances, or CKA at any finite sample size, whereas with weight decay, tied weights, or a small initialization, this agreement was reliable from 212 samples.

Agreement of InfoNCE and Spectral Learners on the Order of Distances Becomes Reliable in the Finite Gibbs Model

Figure in the Theorem 3 card

InfoNCE and spectral learners agreed exactly on the order of all distances among eight evaluation samples in 0 of 96 pairs up to 228 training examples and in 94 of 96 pairs at 234, near the size predicted before the runs.

06Reference

Measures and Terms

Alignment criterion

A test that accepts a pair of models if one relation among shared evaluation samples agrees across the two models within a tolerance under a fixed scale and protocol.

mKNN

Mutual \(k\)-nearest-neighbor overlap, i.e., the fraction of the \(k\) nearest neighbors of each sample shared by the two models, averaged over the samples.

MST overlap

The fraction of the edges of the minimum spanning tree (MST) shared by the two models.

CKA

Linear centered kernel alignment, i.e., the cosine similarity of the two centered Gram matrices, which depends on the distances of each model only up to rescaling.

Calibration

A correction of a score by its values after random permutations of the samples of one model.

Random-pairing baseline

The expected value of a score after a random permutation of the samples of one model, i.e., its chance level.

Size trend

The mean over vision models of the Spearman correlation between a score and the size of the language model.
