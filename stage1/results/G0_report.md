## Notes

- Observed s_N for the slope plants uses all anchors of each nested pool; P1/nulls are cross-fitted: coupling fitted on H1 x H1 pairs of the m=1024 prefix, evaluated on H2 anchors of every m.
- 'oracle' = the same crossing-law (mu) computation with the TRUE P-Gauss coupling (b = u + sigma N(0,1)). Errors shared by P1 and the oracle are properties of the mu-law approximation, not of the coupling estimator.
- N0 = s_N of H1 anchors at m=1024 (full rows); N1 = Gaussian additive noise with sigma from the global affine residual on H1 x H1; N2 = per-anchor Gaussian copula; P2 = (p/4) mean delta_D law with MG Levina-Bickel p-hat at scale max(k,3) (no bias correction).
- Bern plant uses a spherical cap (max D_A ~ 0.16) so that D_A -> D_B stays strictly increasing in float64 (float collisions reported per cell, must be 0).

## Post-run notes (harness-main)

- P-Bern FAIL is one cell of 27: p=8, m=256, beta=1, cross-fitted global-isotonic explained = 0.99778 (in-sample = 1.000000). Exactly one H2xH2 pair lies outside the H1xH1 fit range of D_A, and SPEC section 3's constant extrapolation of g at that pair carries > 99.99% of the cross-fit residual. With beta=1 the warp is nearly linear, so SS0 is only 1% of the variance of D_B and the explained fraction is fragile. All ordinal rungs are exactly identical (Delta = 0) and both CKAs fall strictly with beta in every cell. Every other in-sample and cross-fitted isotonic fraction is >= 0.999. The verdict stands as pre-specified (in-sample AND cross-fitted). SPEC 7's text ('Global-isotonic explained fraction >= 0.999') does not say which; read as in-sample only, P-Bern passes. Decision for the lead.
- P-Gauss FAIL comes from k=1 only (36 cells, max abs err 0.057, P1 biased low by 0.024 on average). At k >= 3 all 154 eligible cells are within 0.02 (max 0.016 at k=3). The SPEC mu-law with the TRUE coupling (oracle) has the same k=1 error (max 0.057), and P1 deviates from the oracle by at most 0.005 on every cell. The coupling estimator is therefore accurate; the k=1 failure belongs to the balance-threshold mu approximation in SPEC 6 (Thm 2 allows |E - mu| <= sqrt(mu/2)). The exact expected discrepancy under the same independent law (diagnostic P1x / oracle_x, cells with <= 400 contributing columns) is within 0.014 at k=1 and 0.006 at k=3. A SPEC/PREREG v1.1 replacing mu by the exact expectation (at least for k <= 3) would pass. Not done here: SPEC and PREREG are frozen.
- P1 is not accurate on smooth non-Gaussian couplings. P-Conf: mean abs err 0.48, with spurious predicted collapse. P-Aniso: 0.05, biased low at small k and large m. Causes: (i) the pooled location-scale coupling gives far columns heavy lower tails, and (ii) heterogeneous anchor couplings are mixed. The null N0 (constant s_N) beats P1 on both classes. For S1.1, P1 failures on pairs whose coupling is smooth/anisotropic should be read in this light.
- P2 (dispersion law) uses the in-sample row-isotonic residual, which is biased low under strong noise (overfitting), and its first-order form can leave [0,1]; predictions are clipped to [0,1] (raw value kept in crossfit output). Its class-mean error is 0.069.
- Levina-Bickel: the per-anchor mean ('lb') is strongly biased at small k (x2 at k=3). The MacKay-Ghahramani average ('mg') is within about 10% for p <= 4 and biased about +20-27% at p=8 (finite-m curvature of the Clifford torus at large k-NN radius).
